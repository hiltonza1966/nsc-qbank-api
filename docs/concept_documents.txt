ABRIDGED CONCEPT DOCUMENT V3
INTRODUCTION
The aim of this concept document is to motivate for the transition from face-to-face setting of all public examinations, viz. General Education Certificate (GEC), Systemic Evaluations (SE) and National Senior Certificate (NSC) Examinations to an item banking system. 
In making this transition, the Department of Basic Education (DBE) envisages that these high-stakes examinations match the high standards adopted by leading assessment bodies worldwide.
As an educational system it is critical that the DBE stays abreast of evolving 21st century practices and incorporates technology into the process of setting examinations as iterated in the DBE’s vision and mission: ‘The vision of the Department of Basic Education is of a South Africa in which all our people will have access to lifelong learning, education and training opportunities. Our mission is to provide leadership in the establishment of a South African education system for the 21st century.’  
In transitioning to an item banking system for the purpose of generating examination papers, the DBE is embracing the role that technology can play in a crucial aspect of the educational process. The aforementioned examinations serve as ‘barometers of the health of the education system’ at the end of each phase. The NSC, being the ultimate examination, is ‘an annual event of major public significance. These examinations signify the culmination of twelve years of formal schooling.’  
BACKGROUND
The responsibility of setting all public examination papers is the remit of the Chief Directorate: National Assessment and Public Examinations (CD: NAPE) which continuously seeks to enhance its examination setting process and the quality of question papers.
Currently, all public examinations are set and moderated face-to-face, at the DBE Head Office in Pretoria. 
Each examination paper is set by a panel of 4-5 examiners, a chief examiner and an internal moderator.
Members of the examining panels are provided with flights, shuttle service, accommodation and catering during their setting processes. These processes necessitate that examiners and moderators have to be hosted in Pretoria for approximately 5 times a year, for at least 4 to 5 days per visit. This has huge cost implications for the DBE.
For the past number of years, a total of approximately 170 NSC and GEC question papers were set and moderated by approximately 400 examiners and moderators.
In recent decades, the DBE has bench-marked its setting processes and NSC examinations against those of leading assessment bodies in other countries. This has led to the realisation that question banks would be a valid alternative to the current approach adopted by DBE panels in the setting of question papers from zero-base on each occasion.  
The DBE, therefore, proposes that a project be implemented to develop an advanced item banking system to generate question papers, tests and marking guidelines for all public examinations (i.e. NSC, GEC and SE) as well as other assessments in the South African school education context, e.g. school-based assessment tasks (SBA).
TRANSITIONING TO AN ITEM BANKING SYSTEM
As previously stated, in order to keep abreast of the changing educational landscape, it is critical that the DBE incorporates technology into its practices. An item banking system, with artificial intelligence (AI) functionality, is a step in this direction. A detailed description of the item banking system that the DBE requires, is captured in the Terms of Reference (ToR) – Appendix A.
For the successful implementation of the item banking project, the following objectives will serve as the focus of the project:
To appoint competent item writers and reviewers to design and review items.
To generate items that are of high quality and standard.
To provide access to quality items, which caters for different types of questions, e.g. multiple-choice/source-based/problem-solving questions.
To decentralise the authoring process through facilitating online collaboration between contracted item-writers, reviewers and moderators at times convenient to them.
IMPLEMENTATION OF THE ITEM BANKING SYSTEM
Prior to the implementation of the item banking system, the following processes have to be completed:
Procurement of a suitable item banking system package and the appointment of a service provider;
Implementation of the software system and enhancements;
Training, certification and appointment of designated users and provision of access; 
Further implications for resources i.e. personnel, physical and financial.
Appointment of a suitable service provider 
This process involves a tender (bidding) process for an item banking system as stipulated in the DBE’s ToR. This process would be preceded by a review of the DBE’s detailed technical documentation together with stipulations of additional customisation outputs to be delivered within the contracted period. 
The successful service provider shall be asked to compile and present a Risk Management Plan and a Security Management Plan to the DBE prior to the commencement of the project.
The service provider will have to provide technical training (to all users) on how to use the system.
Features of the software system and enhancements
	Key features of the software system to be adapted or implemented in advance by the service provider include:
a web-based system designed for desk-top computers and networked computers; 
specifications needed for hardware to be procured by the DBE;
a repository for quality-assured formative and summative assessment items that can be accessed by teachers with tagging of items according to DBE needs e.g. type, difficulty and cognitive levels;
generation of secure tests and question papers for summative assessment for all public examinations and other assessments aligned to the CAPS and Examination Guidelines across the GET and FET bands, according to the question paper generation specifications;
efficiency, security and cost-effectiveness. The large pool of items in an item bank repository allows for question papers or other assessment tasks to be generated in a short space of time.  Items would be based on clear specifications and forwarded to a central point to enable easy retrieval by authorised persons;
a seamless and secure process of tracking inputs from a variety of contracted item writers and reviewers, with audit signals in the unlikely event of breaches;
dual authorisations by code or password and rigorous permissions to access material allocated to item writers or reviewers;
formulation and feedback of psychometric analyses and diagnostics of specific problems; experienced by learners, and a function that redirects teachers and learners to similar tasks or items in a specific content area or topic of items; 
generation of a record of all information relevant for payment of item writers, reviewers and other relevant officials in the item banking process;
a five-year full maintenance, enhancement and support service agreement before the Item Bank system is handed over to DBE;
supporting documentation relating to the item banking system and users;
a training service for the period of the contract with adequate quality-assured material to cover technical, administration, user, management and follow-up training; 
Any other feature as stipulated in the ToR.
Training, certification and appointment of designated users and provision of access  
The DBE will provide an accredited online Short Learning Programme (SLP) on Examination Setting to all applicants interested in applying as an item writer – on condition that they meet the minimum requirements for appointment.
The item banking service provider will be required to conduct training for the period of the contract in collaboration with the DBE, on technical aspects of the software.
All users should first register with a unique username and a secured password. To ensure a high level of security the user registration facility should consist of a two-factor authentication system i.e. one-time password (OTP) or PIN, fingerprint registration or confirmation of identification through a mobile phone number. 
All users will also be trained by the DBE on how to design an item; this will include training on how to tag items with the requisite metadata.
The Internal Moderators will draft both the item specifications and the question paper generation specifications.

ITEM BANKING SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS
It is envisioned that the item banking system will assist the DBE to establish a large pool of items to assist in the generation of examination question papers of comparable standards within limited time frames. This will provide the foundation for the establishment of an item bank for all the subjects, in both the General Education and Training (GET) and the Further Education and Training (FET) bands, from which examination question papers can be generated in future. 
The DBE therefore, has to procure an item banking system which: 
could be leased, maintained and upgraded;
meets 80% of the DBE’s specifications;
allows 20% customisation to the DBE’s requirements;
has AI functionality;
is able to generate question papers, marking guidelines and analysis grids for high-stakes examinations;
allows educators to access formative assessment items, for the generation of SBA tasks.

(a) Accredited item writers will be allowed to draft items offline.

PHASE 3: 
REVIEWING | 3.1 All items that have been uploaded will be reviewed by appointed item reviewers.







3.2 After the first review process, items identified for summative assessment will be reviewed by external reviewers (Umalusi). | 3.1. INTERNAL REVIEW
(a) All items will be reviewed for compliance; validity; etc.
(b) Items will receive a status qualification as either: Accepted, Provisionally Accepted, Accepted with Corrections or Rejected.
(c) Provisionally accepted items will be amended by the item reviewer and Accepted with Corrections will be returned to the item writer, to implement amendments. Rejected items are disposed of.
(d) Thereafter, all Accepted items will be tagged with the necessary metadata.
(e) All items deemed suitable for formative assessment will be provisionally banked and the item banking system will remunerate the item writers accordingly.


3.2 EXTERNAL REVIEW
(a) All items identified for summative assessment will be reviewed by the external reviewers.
(b) Items will receive a status qualification as either: Accepted, Provisionally Accepted, Accepted with Corrections or Rejected.
(c) Provisionally accepted items will be amended by the item reviewer and Accepted with Corrections will be returned to the item writer, to implement amendments. Rejected items are disposed of.
(d) Thereafter, all Accepted items will be tagged with the necessary metadata.
(e) All items deemed suitable for summative assessment will be provisionally banked and the item banking system will remunerate the item writers accordingly.
PHASE 4: 
STORAGE AND MAINTENANCE | All items stored in the repository will be tagged with the relevant metadata. | (a) All items deemed suitable for banking are edited and their tagging is checked.
(b) The item banking system should also be able to generate a register of items that have been banked.
PHASE 5: 
SYSTEM TESTING | 5.1 The DBE will provide the question paper specifications, per subject.


5.2 Practice tests (that closely resemble examination papers) are generated.

Question papers must be constructed in accordance with the identified subjects’ CAPS and Examination Guidelines and the specifications developed by internal moderators.


5.2 PRACTICE TESTS:
The item banking system generates a full examination paper in selected subjects, to be field tested.



5.3 ADMINISTERING THE PRACTICE TEST
(a) The test is administered to 200+ learners as either a formative assessment activity or as a trial examination.
(b) The per item results are submitted to psychometricians for analysis.
(c) Based on the feedback from the psychometricians, items are either accepted or rejected.
PHASE 6: 
QUESTION PAPER GENERATION | 6.1 The Item Banking System will generate full question papers, marking guidelines and analysis grids, using the question paper specifications.

6.2 These question papers will be moderated both internally (DBE) and externally (Umalusi). | 6.1 GENERATION OF QUESTION PAPERS
(a) The item banking system will generate four (i.e. to suit budget phases – needs discussion) full question papers, marking guidelines and analysis grids from the repository, for all subjects. 
(b) Items that are selected will be stored in a separate repository and may not be used within a specified timeframe.


6.2 MODERATION
(a) The internal moderators will moderate the question papers, marking guidelines and analysis grids and effect necessary changes.
(b) The question papers, marking guidelines and analysis grids will be submitted to Umalusi for external moderation.
(c) The internal moderators will effect Umalusi’s recommendations.
PHASE 7: 
PROCESS FINALISATION | 7.1 Once the papers have been approved they will be submitted for editing.
7.2 The DBE will appoint a scrutineer who will write the examination paper and provide feedback. | 7.1 EDITING
All question papers and marking guidelines will be submitted for editing, prior to dissemination to the provinces.

7.2 SCRUTINISING
(a) A scrutineer, with subject expertise and experience, will write the question paper. The marking guideline will then be provided after the paper has been written.
(b) The scrutineer will then provide feedback to the DBE and the internal moderators.
