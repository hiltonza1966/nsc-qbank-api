INTRODUCTION
The Human Sciences Research Council (HSRC) is a Schedule 3A national public entity in terms of the Public Finance Management Act, 1999 (Act No. 1 of 1999 as amended) and the Minister of Science and Technology is the Executive Authority, and the HSRC Board the Accounting Authority of the HSRC. The HSRC is governed by a Board appointed by the Minister of Science and Technology.
The Human Sciences Research Council (HSRC) in collaboration with the Department of Basic Education (DBE) has developed an assessment software called TARMIIfp (Teacher Assessment Resources for Monitoring and Improving Instruction for foundation phase teachers). The software houses a data base of assessment activities and other teaching resources and functionalities that provide opportunities for teachers to diagnose their learners’ weaknesses and strength in specific literacy curriculum areas.  The software was developed on Plone, an open source content management system developed in Python.  
The HSRC is currently looking for a service provider that would be able to continue working from the current system developed and add on modifications to the system based on specifications from the HSRC research team and DBE.

TERMS OF REFERENCE FOR APPOINTMENT OF A SERVICE PROVIDER FOR THE ACQUISITION OF AN ELECTRONIC ITEM BANKING SYSTEM FOR ASSESSMENT AND PUBLIC EXAMINATIONS
AIM
To appoint a suitable service provider for the acquisition of a customised off-the-shelf secure Web-based Item Banking software package, that meets the formative and summative assessment needs of the DBE over a three year period. 
Background Information 
The minister of education is mandated to determine national education policy regarding curriculum frameworks, core syllabuses and education programmes, learner standards, examination and certification and qualifications. The minister is further mandated to determine minimum outcomes and standards, as well as the process and procedures for the assessment of learner achievement in Section 6A of the South African Schools Act, 1996 (Act no. 84 of 1996). The act is applicable to both public and private schools.
Since 2008, NSC an increasing volume of question papers have been developed, moderated and annually administered during the November and March examinations. These question papers are set centrally at the DBE head office in Pretoria. A panel of 3-5 examiners per each subject are responsible for manually setting these question papers. In 2013, 258 question papers were set by the DBE for the November 2013 and March 2014 NSC examination. In the same vein with the rollout of the Annual National Assessments reaching full scale, the setting of ANA tests for 2013 and 2014 involved large panels of examiners, reviewers, versioners and editors. The resultant increase in the number of flights required, and the increase in subsistence, travel costs and accommodation required by the various examination panels are the key cost drivers. 
Item Banking is proposed as an alternative, cost effective approach that will enable the DBE to continue to set ANA tests and NSC examination question papers that are of the appropriate standard. 
The procurement of a viable electronic Item Banking System with a licensing agreement will enable the DBE to develop test specifications and test blueprints from the volume of items that are banked in each subject. Individual item development will also open doors into the psychometrics of ANA and NSC assessment where candidates’ performance can be analysed and understood in terms of the difficulty of an item.  The distance mode of item banking will reduce the cost of bringing examiners into the same physical space. Better use of technology and access to an established DBE Item Bank via web based technology is a future goal. Creative use of technology, change in mindset of having to be in the same physical space as other examiners, and the efficient  use of an electronic system to generate tests and examination question papers will ensure a positive return on investment for the DBE in the near future. 
Item banking will also impact teacher development through improved and iterative formative assessment practice in schools as teachers will have access to sets of items that will be available for use in classroom. Access by teachers to a range available items in the DBE Item Bank is intended to strengthen  both pre-test and post-test remedial interventions in the classroom to enhance the learning experience and improve learners’ academic outcomes. The Item Bank will also be used to develop and store secure items for  the summative examination and assessment purposes of the DBE. 
With the gradual migration to an electronic system of setting the goal is to have items developed and reviewed at a volume that allows for banking so that test and question papers and SBA tasks can be generated from this repository of items. Item Banking will thus facilitate continued opportunities for standard setting and benchmarking by the DBE.  From a cost-benefit perspective Item Banking should be more efficient in terms of time and cost as it is premised on a distance learning approach where items are submitted for review and banking via email. Future thinking will see web based access for item reviewers and any potential item writer to contribute to the repository of required items, from wherever he/she is located, thus minimising the logistical, financial and time contraints experienced in the current centralised approach to examination and assessment question paper development.
SCOPE OF WORK
Preparatory work undertaken with DBE during the initial project phase consisted of the determining of the user and business requirements of the DBE and the compilation of a System Prospectus with an imbedded recommendation to either purchase a custom off-the- shelf Item Banking software package for the DBE. 
Subsequently, a successful service provider will be expected to implement the user and business requirements of the DBE in the development and enhancements required for the delivery and acceptance of a finalised version of an Item Banking system with both formative and summative system that is fully functional in the DBE environment. 
The scope of work over a three year period (August 2016-September 2019) will include the following:  
Conduct a review of DBE project documentation and prepare detailed Technical documentation in response to the scope of work and the outputs to be delivered within the contracted period 
Provide a COTS Item Banking system that meets the business requirements of the DBE.  The DBE is willing to either purchase the full IP of a ready-made system or lease the usage of the Item banking system by means of the payment of an annual license fee.
Depending on the functional fit of the item banking system, an application customisation service is required and must be included to ensure that it can: 
serve as a repository for quality assured ANA and NSC items that can be accessed by teachers; 
is able to generate formative assessment tasks for access by teachers,  
is able to generate secure  items for summative assessment for the NSC and ANA, aligned to CAPS frameworks and assessment and examination guidelines of the DBE across the GET and FET bands; 
generates a payment audit trail of all information relevant to generate claims of respective item developers, item reviewers and all other relevant officials that are active during the system use and review process.
provides diagnostics of learner specific problems.
facilitates standard setting and benchmarking.
calibrate ANA and NSC  items 
provides psychometric analysis of items 
provide the means to modify curriculum frameworks and item allocation when changes to curriculum framework occurs.
A three year maintenance and enhancement service is required from the successful service provider before the item bank system is handed over to DBE for full maintenance and support.
All Item bank system development and user documentation must be made available as part of the system delivery process.
A user training service is also required with adequate training material.  The following levels of training is required:
Administration training,
User training,
Management training, and 
Follow-up training over a one year period.
 This training will be done at the training facilities of the DBE in Pretoria and must include the disciplines of effectiveness and efficiency.  Full training material must be supplied to the users.  The training will be on-going for the period of the contract, one year. All logistical expenses of DBE trainees will be the responsibility of the DBE.
Comprehensive training material that matches the functionality of the Item Banking COTS system must be available at no extra cost to the DBE.  The material will become the property of the DBE and provision must be made to train at least 100 DBE officials.
The successful service provider shall be asked to compile and present a Risk Management Plan and a Security Management Plan to the DBE prior to the commencement of the project.
The major objective of the tender is to purchase application item banking software and the purchasing of hardware and/or system software is excluded. 
Hardware Specifications needed for the system must be indicated.
The price of Item Bank System must also include the cost of populating the system with existing ANA or NSC items that have been used by the DBE I previous tests and examination papers.
